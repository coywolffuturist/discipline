# Adversarial pass

**Brief it to find the failure, not to check the work.**

A reviewer asked "is this correct?" confirms. A reviewer told "this claim is
false, find out why" searches. The second finds things the first cannot.

## What counts

- **FIRED** — an independent, context-free reviewer ran and reported: a specific
  defect, or a certification that it could not find one *after a real attempt*.
- **BLOCKED** — none ran. Say what is therefore UNVERIFIED, in one line.
- **N/A** — the claim is trivial and none of the three categories applies.

Self-review is never FIRED. The author re-reading their own work is the failure
this gate exists to catch.

## The brief

Give it the claim, the access, and the instruction to refute. Give it the
constraints it must not violate. Do **not** give it your reasoning — that is the
context you are trying to exclude.

State what would count as a refutation before it runs, so a null result is
informative rather than reassuring.

## Why it is not optional on the ruled three

Every refuter that has run against a non-trivial claim here has found something,
including on work already break-tested and believed sound: six defects in a
sentinel, three in a money path, and a repository whose scrubbed history was not
the history on its remote. In each case the author had verified the work first.
